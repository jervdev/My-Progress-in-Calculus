03 / BEYOND THE CLASSROOM

Calculus in IT
and Web Development

01

Rate of change

Rate of change shows how quickly something changes. I can relate it to download speed. If 20 MB is downloaded in four seconds, the average speed is 5 MB/s. If the amount of downloaded data is represented as a function of time, its derivative gives the download rate at a specific moment. This helps explain how download speed can increase or decrease.

02

Optimization

Optimization can help find the best option for a task. For example, a system setting may affect how long it takes to process data. If a smooth function represents the processing time, we can use derivatives to find possible minimum points. We then compare these points with the allowed endpoints to find the lowest processing time. This could help us choose a faster setting based on measured results.

03

User-interface animation

I can relate limits and continuity to a menu opening on a website. As the animation ends, the menu’s position should approach its final position. A continuous position function means it does not suddenly jump from one position to another. The derivative of its position gives its velocity. Changing the velocity gradually can make the movement smoother. I can use CSS to create the animation, while Calculus helps explain the movement.

05 / FINAL REFLECTION

What I Learned in Calculus

Before studying Calculus, I thought that this course would involve a lot of formulas and long solutions. With each lesson, I studied functions, limits, and derivatives. Limits were the easiest topic for me, since I could solve the problems using substitution, factoring, and rationalization. I received 47 out of 50 points in the prelim exam. It increased my confidence in the methods I had learned. Moreover, I realized how Calculus works with changes that can be useful for IT and web development.

It was rather hard for me to recall the derivative formulas for trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions. although the formulas are short, there are lots of them to remember. I forgot several of these formulas during the midterm exam and received only 28/50 points. When I knew the formula, I could easily solve a problem, but to recall it on my own was challenging for me.

When I had to write the solution step by step, I could focus more on the rules, signs, and exponents. For example, in Activity #2, my score was 19/25 points. I overthought one of my answers and decided to erase it although it was correct. I should check if there really is any mistake before erasing my work. Also, in Activity #5, I got 45/45 points for solving the problems related to derivatives of radicals, product, and quotient of functions. By rewriting radicals in fractional exponents and applying the suitable rule, I managed to solve these problems.

It is still challenging for me to solve problems with several steps because one wrong sign or forgetting a formula influences the whole result. I have started paying more attention to the process of finding a solution and checking how I found it. The same technique may be used while debugging programs, when you should analyze different aspects of it. Calculus helps explain download speed and movements in website animations. I should practice solving problems without looking at formulas in future lessons.
